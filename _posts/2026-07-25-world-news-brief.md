**U.S. strikes on Iran continue into a second week as the ceasefire collapses, while India's education minister resigns amid mass student protests and oil tops $100/barrel on Red Sea attacks.**

### WORLD NEWS

**Iran-U.S. conflict escalates past two weeks.** A U.S. bombing campaign against Iran has continued for roughly 13 consecutive nights after the June ceasefire (agreed June 17) broke down. Overnight strikes hit Khorramabad, Jask, Ahvaz, Bandar Abbas and Qeshm Island; Iranian media reported four killed and five wounded near Ahvaz. The Pentagon revised its official death toll down from 18 to 14, omitting several recent casualties, drawing criticism over transparency. Trump says an "exit strategy" could come diplomatically or militarily and has warned China and Russia against arming Tehran. Why it matters: this is now a sustained, multi-week U.S.-Iran air war rather than a contained strike, with global energy markets reacting directly. Watch: whether Tehran returns to the negotiating table in Doha/Qatar or the campaign widens. (Bloomberg, CNN, CNBC)

**Oil surges past $100/barrel.** Houthi rebels attacked two Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea on July 23, sending crude above $100 a barrel amid fears the Iran conflict is widening into regional shipping lanes. Tankers are reportedly avoiding the region. Why it matters: a sustained spike raises global inflation risk and squeezes fuel-dependent sectors (see U.S. farm diesel costs below). Watch: further Houthi action and whether Gulf states get pulled deeper into the conflict. (Bloomberg, Washington Post, NBC News)

**Israel launches "wide-scale" West Bank operation.** After a settler-linked clash near the village of Tal (Tell), southwest of Nablus, left two Israelis and four Palestinians dead on July 24, Netanyahu and Defense Minister Israel Katz ordered a large-scale military operation across West Bank villages. Israeli forces arrested roughly 50 Palestinians and raided about 70 homes in Tal on July 25; the home of the attack's alleged perpetrator was slated for demolition. Human Rights Watch has separately warned that a surge in settler violence risks mass atrocities. Why it matters: this marks a sharp escalation in West Bank unrest layered on top of the broader regional conflict. Watch: further IDF raids and international reaction. (Euronews, Al Jazeera, Middle East Monitor, HRW)

### U.S. NEWS

**Iran War costs and Congress pushback.** The escalating Iran conflict has sent diesel prices sharply higher, costing U.S. farmers an estimated $1.4 billion in added fuel costs. The House has approved another War Powers Resolution aiming to constrain Trump's authority to continue strikes without congressional approval. Why it matters: domestic economic and political pressure is building around a war now well into its second week. Watch: Senate action on the War Powers measure and further economic data on fuel/food prices.

**Measles outbreak hits 35-year high.** The CDC confirmed 2,318 measles cases so far in 2026, already surpassing all of 2025 (2,289 cases, three deaths), making this the worst U.S. outbreak in 35 years amid declining vaccination rates. Why it matters: a preventable disease reaching multi-decade case highs signals eroding public health infrastructure and vaccine confidence. Watch: CDC's next case update and any new state-level vaccination mandates or exemptions debate.

**2028 primary calendar takes shape.** Democrats have selected South Carolina to again lead off the 2028 presidential primary calendar, followed by Nevada. Why it matters: an early signal of intra-party positioning well ahead of the cycle. Watch: DNC formal ratification and pushback from Iowa/New Hampshire.

### INDIA NEWS

**Education Minister Dharmendra Pradhan resigns.** Pradhan resigned Saturday and submitted his letter to PM Modi, following weeks of student-led "Cockroach movement" protests over repeated exam paper leaks (including NEET) and demands for accountability from the Education Ministry and National Testing Agency. Protesters at Delhi's Jantar Mantar celebrated the resignation as a major concession, though some say their full list of demands hasn't been met. Why it matters: one of India's largest youth protest movements in recent years has forced out a senior cabinet minister. Watch: who Modi appoints as replacement and whether protesters stand down or press remaining demands. (Bloomberg, Washington Post, Al Jazeera, The Week)

**Sonam Wangchuk ends 26-day hunger strike.** The Ladakh climate activist broke his fast Friday in the presence of Union Ministers J.P. Nadda and Jitendra Singh after weeks of pressure from 65+ MPs and negotiations over Ladakh's demand for statehood and Sixth Schedule protections. Why it matters: defuses (for now) a flashpoint that had drawn national attention to Ladakh's autonomy movement. Watch: whether the government follows through on any commitments made during negotiations.
